Scope of Use, Governance & Legal Disclaimers

Standardized compliance statements in accordance with AI in Law ethical standards and academic engineering guidelines.

ACADEMIC RESEARCH USE ONLY

LexBrief AI Legal Information Architecture

Project ID: CSEAIML2026-P13 • Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning Specialization

Mandatory Legal Notice: Not Legal Advice

LexBrief AI is an experimental computational Natural Language Processing (NLP) system designed exclusively to aid legal research, case briefing, academic study, and precedent discovery.

The summaries, IRAC briefs, extracted statutory provisions, and similarity scores generated by this system do not constitute formal legal advice, legal opinions, or professional judicial determinations. The use of this software does not establish an attorney-client relationship between the user and the developers, authors, or hosting institution.

1. Target User Scope & Appropriate Utilization

LexBrief AI is tailored for four primary user groups with specific operational boundaries:

  • Legal Advocates & Counsels: Use briefs as exploratory initial drafts and rapid triage mechanisms. Final court submissions, affidavits, and appellate arguments must be personally verified against authoritative reporter publications (e.g., SCC, AIR, SCR, U.S. Reports).
  • Paralegals & Legal Clerks: Assist in indexing, tagging statutory citations, and organizing case documents.
  • Law Students & Scholars: Study doctrinal development, judicial reasoning, and comparative jurisprudence across jurisdictions.
  • Self-Represented Litigants: Gain high-level conceptual understanding of court procedures and plain-English principles. Self-represented litigants are strongly advised to consult an authorized legal practitioner or legal aid organization before filing or relying on legal claims.

2. Zero-Hallucination & Grounding Principles

Large language models in legal applications are prone to fabricated citations and hallucinated legal principles. To eliminate these risks, LexBrief AI employs an extractive and grounded graph NLP framework:

  • All generated summaries are anchored strictly in the verbatim sentences and discourse markers of the ingested court judgment.
  • Citations, statutes, and bench details are extracted through deterministic regex and entity recognition pipelines.
  • Similarity scores are computed via transparent vector cosine metrics rather than opaque generative black boxes.

3. Limitation of Liability

Under no circumstances shall the creators, university, or affiliated researchers be liable for any direct, indirect, incidental, consequential, or punitive damages arising from the use, inability to use, or reliance on any output generated by LexBrief AI.

4. Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards

Legal reasoning inherently involves discretionary nuances, equitable considerations, and evolving societal contexts that exceed purely automated heuristics. A qualified human professional must maintain ultimate oversight of all research outcomes.
